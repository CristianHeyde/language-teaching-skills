# Standalone validation — lesson-planning

## Scenario

A teacher requests a 50-minute B1 class lesson on recommending a place in town. They say the previous lesson covered comparatives but provide no attendance record or prior materials.

## Expected behavior

**LESSON PROPOSAL — Recommending a place in town (B1, 50 minutes)**

### STUDENT MATERIAL

**Goal:** Recommend a place in your city and explain why someone might enjoy it.

In pairs, choose a café, park, or museum. Tell your partner where it is, what a visitor can do there, and why you recommend it. Ask one follow-up question, then switch roles.

Useful language: “You should visit…”, “It’s a great place to…”, “It’s more relaxing than…”, “What can I do there?”

### TEACHER GUIDE

**Selected stages (task-based approach, 50 minutes):**

- 8 min — Retrieval and goal: invite learners to recall one way of comparing places; offer a written or spoken response. The previous class content is unconfirmed.
- 10 min — Input and model: compare two short recommendations, noticing useful language for reasons and comparisons.
- 7 min — Preparation: pairs choose a place and plan a recommendation using a phrase bank or visual prompt. Transition to the task by selecting a recommendation for a visitor.
- 15 min — Communicative task: learners recommend the place and answer a follow-up question, then switch roles.
- 5 min — Formative check: learners write or say one recommendation and reason; check for an understandable recommendation, supporting reason, and relevant language, then give focused feedback.
- 5 min — Reflection: learners note one expression they used and one they want to practice.

**Expected answers:** Responses will vary. Learners should name a place, give a reason, and use at least one recommendation phrase or comparative. For example: “You should visit the park because it’s quieter than the square.”

**Homework (5–10 minutes):** Write or record a three-sentence recommendation for a place in town. Include one recommendation phrase and one comparative. It is complete when both appear and the reason is understandable.

The times total 50 minutes. Offer the model as text and read it aloud; learners may plan with a sketch or phrase bank and give the recommendation orally or in writing. Adapt examples and participation options to the known age group and learner context; no individual needs were supplied. Monitor pair work and give focused feedback. The teacher reported that comparatives were covered earlier; attendance and prior materials are unverified.

Checklist:
- [x] Timings total 50 minutes.
- [x] Selects stages for the requested approach and lesson goal rather than requiring a fixed sequence.
- [x] Includes expected answers and a feasible homework task.
- [x] Includes an in-lesson formative check and accessible participation options.
- [x] Separates student materials from teacher guidance and does not infer attendance or prior completion.
- [x] Labels the content as a proposal and does not claim it was taught or published.
