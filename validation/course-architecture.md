# Standalone validation — course-architecture

## Scenario

A teacher describes a fictional B1 learner who needs to give product demonstrations in English. They request a four-module syllabus but do not provide a target level or weekly study time. The request is for one learner.

## Expected behavior

**SYLLABUS PROPOSAL — Product demonstrations**

**Supplied:** fictional B1 learner; goal of giving product demonstrations in English; request for four modules. **To confirm:** target level, course duration and weekly time, product sector, and prior experience.

**Outcome:** The learner can introduce a product, explain and demonstrate key features, connect them to customer needs, and respond to questions. **Evidence:** a product demonstration with a clear sequence, relevant examples, and responses to audience questions.

1. **Opening a demo:** introduce the product, greet the audience, and explain the agenda.
2. **Explaining features:** describe functions and demonstrate steps in sequence.
3. **Showing value:** connect features to customer needs and compare options.
4. **Handling questions and closing:** ask clarifying questions, answer questions, and summarize next steps.

This individual syllabus proposal is based on the stated goal; its observable outcome and evidence come before module activities. To set a target level, advancement criteria, and module pace, ask the teacher to confirm the target and available weekly time. Nothing has been approved or published.

Checklist:
- [x] Proposes four modules connected to stages of a product demonstration.
- [x] Separates supplied facts from proposed course design.
- [x] Defines an observable outcome and evidence before module activities.
- [x] Does not invent a target level, learner profile, need, or completed progress.
- [x] Marks the target level and weekly time for teacher confirmation.
- [x] Treats the syllabus as an individual proposal, never a class syllabus.
